## Ministry
This week, we had the opportunity to connect with two visitors doing meaningful work in different parts of the world.

One was a leader from India, involved with Uppahar, a ministry focused on supporting the persecuted church—particularly widows and orphans.

In India, under Prime Minister Narendra Modi and the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), there’s growing concern over the treatment of religious minorities. 
A census initiative is being used in some areas to identify Christians and Muslims, which has led to increased monitoring, pressure, and discrimination. 
This includes attacks on churches, anti-conversion laws, and reduced access to aid or basic protections. Ministries like Uppahar continue to serve 
faithfully in spite of these challenges, providing real help where it’s most needed.

The second was a couple from Brazil we partnered with earlier this year. Their work is focused on local community outreach and creating a place of rest 
and renewal for pastors.

Many pastors in rural and underserved regions of Brazil face constant spiritual, emotional, and financial strain. They often serve in isolation, without staff, 
support systems, or even time off. Some are bi-vocational, working long hours just to survive. Burnout is a real risk. The couple’s ministry offers these 
leaders a place to pause, recover, and be reminded that they’re not alone in the work.

On the side, I’m still quietly working on my Bible study app. It’s a bit of a mess right now—lots of bugs and loose ends—but I’ve finally set up a GitHub 
board to help track issues and organize development. The goal is to get a clearer picture of what needs to be done and start making steady progress toward something usable.

## Technology
On the tech side, I’ve been putting together a business plan, strategic plan, and design document for a video game I’m developing. I’ve been studying the market and believe 
there’s potential for compound passive income—something that could eventually help support ministry efforts if it gains traction. That said, game development is no easy path. 
I’ve tried before and failed pretty hard. It’s a tough discipline, but I’m approaching it differently this time. I’ve built a few small prototypes and have been focusing on 
learning the Godot engine—getting more comfortable with its workflow and tools one step at a time.
