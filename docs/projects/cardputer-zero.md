# Cardputer Zero: getting started with LoRa and GNSS

The handheld in the reference photo is labeled **Cardputer Zero**. It has a small screen, a physical keyboard, an external antenna, and markings for **LoRa** and **GNSS**. Think of it as a pocket-sized radio terminal. The exact radio band, board revision, and installed firmware need to be confirmed on the device or from its seller.

## What it can do

- **LoRa** carries small amounts of data over radio. With compatible firmware and another compatible node, you can try sending short text messages without Wi-Fi or cellular service. If the firmware supports mesh routing, other nodes can relay messages.
- **GNSS** receives satellite signals to work out your location. Compatible firmware can display your position and may let you send it over LoRa. Location sharing is worth checking before joining a public network.
- **The keyboard and screen** make it possible to compose and read messages directly on the handheld when the firmware provides a standalone messaging interface.

It can be useful for learning about radio, building a small network with friends, or trying simple check-ins while camping. Terrain, buildings, antenna placement, and other nodes affect whether a message gets through. Bring a dependable means of communication for emergencies.

## Try a first message

1. **Identify the hardware and firmware.** Check the seller's listing, board markings, and the on-device About or Settings screen. If the device came with working firmware, start with that. Before flashing anything, confirm support for the exact board and radio module. [MeshCore](https://github.com/meshcore-dev/MeshCore) and [Meshtastic](https://meshtastic.org/) are examples of LoRa projects with hardware compatibility requirements.
2. **Set up two compatible nodes.** Attach the antenna intended for your radio band before transmitting. Use the same firmware family and compatible radio and chat settings on both devices. Select the region setting appropriate for where you operate.
3. **Send a message nearby.** With both nodes powered on, send a short message and have the other person reply. Once messages work nearby, try again outdoors with some separation.
4. **Check GNSS outdoors.** Give the device a clear view of the sky and wait for a position fix. Check the coordinates on the screen before enabling any position sharing.

## Where to buy and what to budget

Search for **Cardputer Zero GNSS LoRa** and confirm the seller's photos and part numbers match the unit you want. Ask whether the price includes the handheld, radio and GNSS hardware, antenna, battery, and case. If the radio is sold separately, check its connector and supported frequency band before ordering.
