### **What Respect Looks Like When Dealing with Toxic People**

**Main Theme**

> The world says respect is earned. God commands His people to give honor where honor is due—not because people deserve it, but because we serve Christ. Respect is a reflection of our character, not theirs. Yet biblical respect never requires us to participate in sin, enable abuse, or submit to evil.

---

## Outline (45 Minutes)

**Introduction (5 min)**

* "Respect is earned."
* Is that actually biblical?
* Difference between respect as the world defines it vs. biblical honor.
* Respect reflects Christ.
* Respect does **not** equal agreement.
* Respect does **not** equal trust.
* Respect does **not** equal obedience when obedience would be sin.

---

### Part 1 — Respect is Commanded, Not Earned (10 min)

Primary passages:

Romans 13:1-7

1 Peter 2:13-18

Titus 3:1-2

Ecclesiastes 10:20

Examples

David toward Saul

Joseph toward Pharaoh

Daniel toward Nebuchadnezzar

Paul toward Roman officials

Key statement:

> God never says "Honor good rulers."

He simply says honor those in authority.

The Roman government under Nero was hardly righteous.

Peter still wrote:

> "Honour all men. Love the brotherhood. Fear God. Honour the king."
> —1 Peter 2:17

That king was Nero.

Application:

Respecting authority says more about your relationship with God than theirs.

---

### Part 2 — Respect Does Not Mean Obeying Evil (10 min)

Acts 5:29

"We ought to obey God rather than men."

Examples

Hebrew midwives

Daniel

Shadrach, Meshach, Abednego

Peter and John

Notice something:

None of them became disrespectful.

Daniel didn't insult Nebuchadnezzar.

The three Hebrews didn't mock the king.

Peter wasn't sarcastic.

Instead:

"We cannot do what you ask."

Respectful.

Firm.

Unmovable.

Application

You can say:

"No."

without saying:

"You're an idiot."

### Part 4 — Respect Does Not Mean Remaining in Abuse (10 min)

This is where many sermons become unbalanced.

Differentiate:

Respect

Submission

Trust

Reconciliation

These are not synonyms.

Examples

David fled Saul.

Repeatedly.

He respected Saul.

He would not kill Saul.

But he also would not stay in the palace.

Jesus often withdrew.

Paul escaped Damascus.

Paul appealed to Caesar.

Paul used Roman citizenship.

Joseph fled Potiphar's wife.

There is no biblical virtue in standing still while someone hurts you.

Discuss:

Domestic abuse

Physical abuse

Emotional abuse

Repeated manipulation

Threats

Stalking

Explain carefully:

A Christian may leave.

A Christian may call the police.

A Christian may establish boundaries.

A Christian may seek safety.

Respecting someone does not require remaining within reach of their sin.

Mention Romans 12:

"If it be possible..."

Sometimes peace is not possible.

Sometimes distance is.

---

### Part 5 — What Respect Looks Like with Toxic People (8 min)

Very practical.

Respect looks like:

Not insulting.

Not slandering.

Speaking truth calmly.

Setting boundaries.

Leaving dangerous situations.

Refusing revenge.

Praying for them.

Not rejoicing when they fall.

Romans 12

Matthew 5

Proverbs 24:17

David is the perfect illustration.

He fled.

He cried.

He hid.

He defended himself.

Yet when Saul died:

He mourned.

He never celebrated.

That is biblical respect.

---

## Conclusion

Repeat:

Respect is not agreement.

Respect is not approval.

Respect is not trust.

Respect is not submission to sin.

Respect is not remaining in abuse.

Respect is treating people in a manner worthy of Christ regardless of how they treat us.

End with Jesus.

No one was treated more unjustly.

He was mocked.

Spit upon.

Beaten.

Crucified.

Yet He never reviled in return.

1 Peter 2:23

> "Who, when he was reviled, reviled not again..."

That does not mean He approved of evil.

He called sin exactly what it was.

But He did so without becoming sinful Himself.

Elders, for example, are said to be "counted worthy of double honour" (1 Timothy 5:17), indicating that "honor" can have different expressions depending on the relationship.
