### **What Respect Looks Like When Dealing with Toxic People**

**Main Theme**
> The world says respect is earned. God commands His people to give honor where honor is due—not because people deserve it, but because we serve Christ and people are made in the image of Christ. Respect is a reflection of our character, not theirs. Yet, Biblical respect never requires us to participate in sin, enable abuse, or submit to evil.

---

## Outline (45 Minutes)
**Introduction (5 min)**

* "Respect is earned."
* Is that actually Biblical?
* Difference between respect as the world defines it vs. Biblical honor.
* Respect reflects Christ.
* Respect does **not** equal agreement.
* Respect does **not** equal trust.
* Respect does **not** equal obedience when obedience would be sin.

---

### Part 1 — Respect is Commanded, Not Earned (10 min)
- Primary Passages:
- **Romans 13:1-7** — God establishes earthly authorities, and believers are commanded to respect and submit to their lawful authority because resisting rightful authority is ultimately resisting God's order.
- **1 Peter 2:13-18** — Christians are instructed to honor governing authorities and even difficult masters so that their Godly conduct points others to Christ rather than bringing reproach upon the Gospel.
- **Titus 3:1-2** — Paul reminds believers to be obedient, peaceable, gentle, and respectful toward all people, demonstrating the transforming work of Christ in everyday life.
- **Ecclesiastes 10:20** — God warns us to guard even our private thoughts and conversations about rulers because disrespect has a way of spreading and revealing the condition of our hearts.

*Examples:*
- **David toward Saul** — David refused to kill King Saul, calling him "the LORD'S anointed," even though Saul repeatedly tried to murder him (1 Samuel 24; 26).
- **Joseph toward Pharaoh** — Joseph faithfully served Pharaoh with wisdom and integrity, recognizing that God had placed him there to preserve many lives (Genesis 41).
- **Daniel toward Nebuchadnezzar** — Daniel spoke respectfully to a proud pagan king while faithfully delivering God's difficult messages without compromise (Daniel 2; 4).
- **Paul toward Roman officials** — Paul treated governors, kings, and military leaders with courtesy and respect even while unjustly imprisoned and standing trial (Acts 24-26).

*Key statement:*
> God never says "Honor good rulers."
He simply says honor those in authority.
The Roman government under Nero was hardly righteous.
> Peter still wrote:
> "Honour all men. Love the brotherhood. Fear God. Honour the king."
> —1 Peter 2:17
That king was Nero.

**Application:**
> Respecting authority says more about your relationship with God than theirs.

### Part 2 — Respect Does Not Mean Obeying Evil (10 min)
**Acts 5:29**
> "We ought to obey God rather than men."
Examples:
- Hebrew midwives
- Daniel
- Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah (Shadrach, Meshach, Abednego)
- Peter and John
*Notice something:*
- None of them became disrespectful.
- Daniel didn't insult Nebuchadnezzar.
- The three Hebrews didn't mock the king.
- Peter wasn't sarcastic.
*Instead:*
> "We cannot do what you ask."
* Respectful.
* Firm.
* Unmovable.

**Application**
> You can say: "No."
> Without saying: "You're an idiot."

### Part 4 — Respect Does Not Mean Remaining in Abuse (10 min)
This is where many sermons become unbalanced.
*Differentiate:*
- Respect
- Submission
- Trust
- Reconciliation
These are not synonyms.
**Examples:**
- David fled Saul.
   - Repeatedly.
   - He respected Saul.
   - He would not kill Saul.
   - But he also would not stay in the palace.
- Jesus often withdrew.
- Paul escaped Damascus.
   - Paul appealed to Caesar.
   - Paul used Roman citizenship.
- Joseph fled Potiphar's wife.
**There is no Biblical virtue in standing still while someone hurts you.**
*Discuss:*
- Domestic abuse
- Physical abuse
- Emotional abuse
- Repeated manipulation
- Threats
- Stalking
*Explain carefully:*
- A Christian may leave.
- A Christian may call the police.
- A Christian may establish boundaries.
- A Christian may seek safety.
**Application:**
>Respecting someone does not require remaining within reach of their sin.
* Romans 12:18
* "If it be possible..."
Sometimes peace is not possible.
Sometimes distance is.

### Part 5 — What Respect Looks Like with Toxic People (8 min)
- Very practical.
*Respect looks like:*
- Not insulting.
- Not slandering.
- Speaking truth calmly.
- Setting boundaries.
- Leaving dangerous situations.
- Refusing revenge.
- Praying for them.
- Not rejoicing when they fall.
* Romans 12
* Matthew 5
* Proverbs 24:17
David is the perfect illustration.
- He fled.
- He cried.
- He hid.
- He defended himself.
Yet when Saul died:
- He mourned.
- He never celebrated.
That is Biblical respect.
**Application:**
> Respect is not saying, "You are right." It is saying, "I will honor God by the way I treat you."
---

## Conclusion
*Repeat:*
- Respect is not agreement.
- Respect is not approval.
- Respect is not trust.
- Respect is not submission to sin.
- Respect is not remaining in abuse.
- Respect is treating people in a manner worthy of Christ regardless of how they treat us.
End with Jesus.
- No one was treated more unjustly.
- He was mocked.
- Spit upon.
- Beaten.
- Crucified.
Yet, He never reviled in return.
**1 Peter 2:23**
> "Who, when he was reviled, reviled not again..."
That does not mean He approved of evil.
He called sin exactly what it was.
But He did so without becoming sinful Himself.
The world says, "Treat people the way they treat you."
Jesus says, "Treat people the way I treat you."
**Final Application:**
> Don't ask, "Who deserves my respect?" Ask, "How can I honor Christ in the way I treat others?"

Elders, for example, are said to be "counted worthy of double honour" (1 Timothy 5:17), indicating that "honor" can have different expressions depending on the relationship.
